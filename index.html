How flipping works

Flipping means buying an item on the Grand Exchange for less than other players will pay, then selling it back for more. Your profit is the gap between the two prices (the margin) minus the GE tax, multiplied by how many you trade.

Reading the prices table

Buy at
The latest price someone instantly sold at. Players dumping items fill buy offers here, so this is roughly where you place your buy offer.
Sell at
The latest price someone instantly bought at. Impatient buyers pay this, so this is roughly where you list your sell offer.
Margin
Sell at − buy at − tax. This is profit per item if both offers fill at those prices.
ROI
Margin ÷ buy price. Tells you how hard your money is working, regardless of item price.
Volume / hr
How many traded in the last hour (from the OSRS Wiki). Low volume means your offers may sit for hours, or never fill.
Buy limit
The most you can buy of an item in a 4-hour window, starting from your first purchase.
Potential
Margin × how many you can buy (your buy limit, or fewer if your cash stack can't cover it). It's a ceiling, not a promise.
Updated
How long since the older of the two prices last traded. Stale prices (over ~30 minutes) are often fake margins.

A safe way to start

  1. Pick high-volume items. Consumables like runes, food, potions, arrows and logs trade thousands per hour. Margins are small, but offers fill fast and the risk is low.
  2. Check the margin yourself. Before committing, buy one item at a high price (it fills instantly at the real sell price), then sell one at a low price (it fills at the real buy price). That shows the true current margin.
  3. Place your offers inside the margin. Buy a little above "buy at" and sell a little below "sell at" to get filled sooner.
  4. Always count the tax. A 2% tax wipes out any flip with an ROI under about 2%. This app already subtracts it.
  5. Spread your cash. Use several GE slots on different items instead of putting your whole stack into one.
  6. Log every flip. Use the Flip log tab to see what actually made money, not what looked good.

Common mistakes

  • Chasing an item whose price just spiked. You usually buy at the top.
  • Trusting a huge margin on a low-volume item. Often only one trade happened, hours ago.
  • Forgetting the buy limit and leaving cash idle for 4 hours.
  • Ignoring game updates. New content and patch notes can move prices quickly.

Where the data comes from

Prices come from the OSRS Wiki's real-time price API, which collects trades reported by RuneLite players. They refresh every minute here. They're a close estimate, not the exact state of the GE order book. See the OSRS Wiki real-time prices page.
